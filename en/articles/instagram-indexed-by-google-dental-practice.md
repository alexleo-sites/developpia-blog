Public posts from an Instagram professional account can appear in Google and Microsoft Bing results. For a dental practice, a line has just moved. What you post on Instagram is no longer read only by your followers. It has become a page Google can show to a patient looking for a treatment in your town.

In other words, social content now feeds into SEO, meaning the work that decides where you rank on Google. We opened Instagram's Help Center and copied the text, because the summaries going around contradict each other on one specific point: what happens to Stories.

## What Instagram's Help Center says, word for word

The Help Center article [Why can search engines index public Instagram photos and videos?](https://help.instagram.com/147542625391305) states: "we allow search engines to index photos and videos from public posts and reels uploaded or published since January 1, 2020." Reels are Instagram's short videos.

Three conditions must all be met at the same time, and the text puts them this way:

- "The account holder is currently over 18."
- "The account is currently public."
- "The account is currently a professional account."

So a private account is not affected. Neither is a personal account. A practice account switched to professional mode and left public is. Professional mode is the one that gives you access to insights and clickable contact details.

The Help Center adds an important detail: if you let others reshare or remix your content, "the remixed or reshared version of your content may be indexed and appear in search engine results." So your post can show up on Google from someone else's account.

## Stories, Highlights, and what stays out

Stories are the posts that disappear after 24 hours, unless you save them to Highlights. This is the point on which the summaries contradict each other. Instagram first says it "generally asks search engines like Google and Microsoft Bing not to index photo and video content from stories, reels and posts, as well as highlights content." Then it makes an exception for accounts that meet all three conditions. And within that exception, Stories saved to Highlights are named: "all photos and videos from public reels, story highlights and posts shared by the account may be indexed."

| Content from a public professional account | Can be indexed | What the Help Center says |
|---|---|---|
| Post published since January 1, 2020 | Yes | "uploaded or published since January 1, 2020" |
| Public reel published since that date | Yes | same sentence, "public posts and reels" |
| Story saved to Highlights | Yes | "public reels, story highlights and posts" |
| Story that disappears after 24 hours | Not named in the exception | Instagram "generally asks" search engines not to index it |
| Private account, or personal account | No | the three conditions are not met |

Remember the third row. A set of Stories pinned to your profile, for example "First visit" or "Access and parking," is not ephemeral content as far as Google is concerned. The Help Center groups it with posts, among what can be indexed.

> **Key takeaway**
> An indexed post becomes a page Google can show for a long time. Instagram's Help Center notes that removal from a third-party search engine's index can take time, "even if you delete your account."

## Why this brings Instagram into your SEO

A patient does not type the name of your practice. They type a treatment and a town, into the search engine that handles 88.76% of searches in France (Statcounter, August 2026). In 2025, 59.8% of people in France looked for health information online (Eurostat, 2025).

An Instagram post could not answer those searches as long as it stayed out of search engines. Now it can appear there. A post that explains the steps of an implant placement, with the practice name and town written out in the caption, becomes one more candidate on that results page. It is the same mechanism that brings up your treatment pages, and we described it in our article on [how Google picks the local pack (the three practices shown on the map)](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dentist-near-me-google-maps-local-pack/).

There is a second effect, in the other direction. A patient who already has your name checks you out before calling, and often does so on a social network: in the first half of 2025, one in two people in France ran at least one search through a social network (Médiamétrie, 2025, survey of 3,000 people aged 15 to 80). An empty practice account, or an account where nothing comes up, is a check that falls flat.

A note in the interest of honesty. No public source measures the share of patients who choose their dentist based on a social network. The percentages going around on this have no open source.

## The check to run yourself, in three steps

Three steps, on your phone, in two minutes.

1. Open Instagram, go to your profile, open the menu, then your account settings.
2. Look for account privacy. That is where the Help Center points you: it tells you to go "to your account's privacy settings to turn off your public photos and videos appearing in search engine results." Note the direction: the text only explains how to turn it off. So a public professional account is included without having asked for anything.
3. On Google, type "site:instagram.com" followed by your account name, with no space after the colon. You will see the list of what is already indexed. Google's help center describes these [search operators](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/2466433?hl=en).

If nothing comes up, there are two explanations: the setting is turned off, or Google has not visited those pages yet. Run the test again two weeks later.

## What it changes about what you post

A post read by a follower and the same post read by a stranger arriving from Google are not the same text. The second reader has no context. They land on an image and three lines of caption.

Four practical consequences:

- **The caption has to stand on its own.** The treatment, the town and the practice name written out plainly, not implied. A caption that starts with "as promised yesterday" means nothing to a stranger.
- **The rules of the French Dental Council (Ordre national des chirurgiens-dentistes) apply to the letter.** They do not change because the text is on a social network. Article R.4127-215-1 requires fair and honest communication, with no third-party testimonials, no comparison with other dentists and no encouragement of unnecessary treatment. [Decree No. 2020-1658 of December 22, 2020](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000042730959) opened the door to information; it did not open the door to advertising. The lever-by-lever details are in our article on [the visibility levers that remain allowed](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dentist-advertising-ban-what-is-allowed/).
- **Professional secrecy becomes more sensitive, not less.** The recommendations of the Dental Council's National Council state: "As soon as a patient is filmed inside a dental practice, they must under no circumstances be identifiable. Their face must be blurred even if the patient wishes to give express consent to the use of their image." An indexed image no longer lives in a scrolling feed; it lives on a results page.
- **Your bio follows the same rules as your website.** The titles, degrees and positions shown at the top of your profile are professional communication. We listed [the information you are allowed to display](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/titles-degrees-specialties-on-a-dentist-website/), and the list applies to three lines of bio as well.

Our working rule is simple: no post goes out without the dentist's written sign-off. The dentist is the one accountable to the Dental Council, not the service provider.

## What indexing does not do

Four limits to keep in mind.

Indexing does not replace your website or your [Google Business Profile](https://en.developpia.fr/google-business-profile-dentist/). An indexed post is one more page, with three lines of text. Your treatment page answers the patient's question in full.

It cannot be bought. Article R.4127-217 II prohibits obtaining, "for payment or by any other means, a digital listing that gives priority display to information about the dentist in the results of a search carried out on the internet," and the Dental Council specifies "paid or not." This setting opens a door; it does not decide where you rank.

It only concerns Instagram. The Help Center text says nothing about any other network.

Finally, it does not excuse you from the question of machine readability. The principle is the same as for your website: content a bot cannot read does not exist for it, and we detailed that path in the article on [the four walls that make a dentist website invisible to AI](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/why-ai-cannot-read-your-dental-website/).

So the question is no longer whether a practice should be on Instagram, but what it is allowed to post there, post by post. ## Frequently asked questions

### Should I turn off indexing for my practice's Instagram account?

That is the dentist's decision, and it should be made with the scope in mind. Leaving indexing on makes your informational posts findable on Google; turning it off limits your account to your followers. Before deciding, go back through your old posts: they are the ones that will come up, and Instagram's Help Center notes that removal from a third-party search engine's index can take time.

### Are my old posts affected?

Yes, those published since January 1, 2020. Instagram's Help Center states that it allows indexing of photos and videos "from public posts and reels uploaded or published since January 1, 2020." So a 2021 post you forgot about can show up in a search today, with its caption exactly as it is.

### Can an Instagram post replace a page on my website?

No. A post carries an image and a few lines, on a platform whose rules and display you do not control. A treatment page on your website explains the steps, duration, aftercare, alternatives and fees, and it belongs to you. Instagram indexing adds a way in; it does not move the house.
