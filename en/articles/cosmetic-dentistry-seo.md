Take a fictional practice, the Saint-Julien practice in Tours. Three dentists, recent technical equipment, a practice focused on veneers, whitening and clear aligners. On the practice name, the website comes up first. On "veneers Tours", it is nowhere to be found, and the patients who walk in for cosmetic work all come by word of mouth.

This situation comes up in almost every practice building a cosmetic side. The reason is simple: SEO for a cosmetic dentistry practice does not work like SEO for a general practice. The patient is not in pain, is not in a hurry, and is not looking for the nearest practice. Here is what that changes, in concrete terms.

## A patient who compares for weeks, and comes from far away

A patient with a raging toothache wants an appointment today, less than ten minutes away. A patient considering veneers has been thinking about it for six months. They read, they compare, they put it off, they come back. And they are willing to drive an hour to a practice that reassured them.

That difference shifts everything about SEO (being found online). On the Google map, distance is one of the three official criteria, along with relevance and prominence (Google help). A patient from Blois will therefore never see your Tours practice in the local pack (the three practices shown on the map). They will, however, read your pages, because a well-written page ranks with no distance condition, and because AI tools rely on those pages to answer.

The cosmetic patient's journey runs mostly through the written word. 59.8% of French people look for health information online (Eurostat, 2025), Google handles 88.76% of searches in France (Statcounter, August 2026), and 56.6% of French people already use artificial intelligence, including to find a dentist (Arcom and Médiamétrie, April 2026). ## The words they type, and what each word expects from you

The dentist says "cosmetic dentistry", "ceramic restoration", "adhesive dentistry". The patient says "veneers", "white teeth", "crooked top teeth". The patient's word goes into your titles and page addresses; the dentist's word stays in the text, explained.

Each family of searches expects a specific answer, and that is where most websites stop too early.

| What the patient types | What they really want to know | What the page must contain |
|---|---|---|
| dental veneers | Is it permanent, will my teeth be filed down | The process, what is removed or not, how long they last, aftercare |
| dental veneers cost | How much, and what is included | A fee range, what it covers, the treatment estimate |
| in-office teeth whitening | How is it different from a kit bought online | The in-office method, contraindications, how long the result lasts |
| clear aligners for adults | How long, will people notice | The process, the duration, follow-up, cases that call for another treatment |
| broken front tooth | A quick, discreet fix | Composite, veneer, crown, with the limits of each |
| fixing my front teeth | Where to start, who to talk to | How the first consultation goes, the exam, the treatment plan |

This table reads both ways. On the left, what you write in your titles. On the right, what must appear in the text, or the patient goes elsewhere to find the missing answer.

## One page per treatment, written like a first consultation

The principle is still [one page per treatment](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/one-page-per-treatment-dentist-website/): a "cosmetic dentistry" page that lists everything in one line ranks for nothing. Veneers, whitening, aligners, composite bonding: four different searches, four pages.

Each page follows the same outline, in the order the patient asks their questions:

- who the treatment is for, and who it is not for;
- the process step by step, with what happens at each appointment;
- the total treatment time and the number of visits;
- what is removed, polished or preserved on the tooth, in plain words;
- recovery, possible sensitivity, and aftercare;
- the alternatives, with the limits of each;
- the fees, as a range, with a reminder that the treatment estimate sets the final amount.

Information on fees is not optional: article R.4127-240 II requires dentists to inform patients about their fees and payment methods, and the order of May 30, 2018 sets the minimum list of procedures to display. We have covered this presentation in detail in the article [fees on your dental practice website](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dental-fees-on-your-website-rules/). In cosmetic dentistry, it is often the question that decides the call, and the practice that addresses it openly wins appointments over the one that avoids it. Our [dental practice website](https://en.developpia.fr/dental-practice-website/) page shows where these pages fit among the others.

> **The key takeaway**
> In cosmetic dentistry, patients choose the practice that answered their questions before they dared to ask them. A page that explains the process, the limits and the fees informs, sells nothing, and stays exactly within what the code allows.

## What you show, when you don't show mouths

This is the question that comes up most often in cosmetic dentistry. The [recommendations of June 19, 2026](https://www.ordre-chirurgiens-dentistes.fr/download/109355/) from the National Council of the French Dental Council (Ordre national des chirurgiens-dentistes) mention before-and-after photos in the conditional, in a list of examples that would tend to suggest a guaranteed result. The code itself never names them. In Luxembourg, however, they are banned.

The cautious approach is therefore not to build an entire page on smile comparisons, and to give patients other ways to picture themselves:

- the technical equipment: digital impressions, intraoral camera, partner dental lab, materials used;
- the simulation shown during the consultation, described without being promised as a result;
- the dentist's background, recognized degrees and training in cosmetic dentistry;
- explanatory diagrams, a photo of the treatment room, the team.

Two rules frame everything else. Communication must be fair and honest, with no third-party testimonials and no comparison with other dentists (article R.4127-215-1), which rules out patient reviews copied onto the veneers page. And article R.4127-217 II forbids obtaining, for payment or by any other means, a listing that would make your information appear first: the decree is [available on Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000042730959). The guide [what the Dental Council allows on a dentist website](https://en.developpia.fr/guides/what-the-dental-council-allows-on-a-dentist-website/) goes through each rule, and the article on [the visibility levers that are still allowed](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dentist-advertising-ban-what-is-allowed/) shows what the 2020 decree opened up.

## The Google Business Profile, even when the patient comes from far away

The profile will not bring you the patient from Blois, but it decides what they see the day they look up your name. Three fields matter especially in cosmetic dentistry.

Services, first: "veneers", "whitening", "clear aligners" should be listed in the patient's words, not yours. Then the description, which uses those same words. Finally the photos, which show the practice and the technical equipment. Address, phone number and hours stay identical to the website, word for word. Our [Google Business Profile for dentists](https://en.developpia.fr/google-business-profile-dentist/) page lists every field.

A note on reviews, since cosmetic work naturally attracts them. No text forbids a patient from leaving a review, but in 2025 the Dental Council asked for reviews of dentists to be removed. The safe approach is never to organize collecting them and to reply neutrally, or not at all. Everything is detailed in the article [Google reviews and the code of ethics](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/google-reviews-dental-practice-code-of-ethics/).

## AI search, where the price question gets asked

Since July 22, 2026, Google has been showing its AI Overviews and AI Mode in France, which answer before the list of websites. A patient hesitating over veneers often asks the whole question at once: how much it costs, whether it damages the teeth, which practice near them does it. These answers rely on readable pages.

Two settings make a website invisible to these bots. The bots from OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity do not read JavaScript, the small program that fills in the page after it opens: a page displayed this way does not exist for them. And Cloudflare, a service that protects many websites, has been blocking AI bots by default since July 2025. The guide [how AI chooses a dentist](https://en.developpia.fr/guides/how-ai-chooses-a-dentist/) gives the steps to follow. Presence in these answers is measured as the percentage of answers in which the practice is cited, never as a ranking.

## The ten-minute check, to run yourself

Take your phone, turn off Wi-Fi, and go through these six points on your own website.

1. Search "veneers" followed by the name of your city. See who comes up, and whether one of your pages appears.
2. Open your website and look for a page dedicated to veneers, with its own address. If the treatment is just one line on a "treatments" page, nothing can rank.
3. On that page, count how many of the seven items in the outline above are actually there.
4. Look for the fee range. If it is missing, the patient will go looking for it at another practice.
5. Check that the practice phone number is tappable on that page, not just on the home page.
6. Ask an AI tool where to get veneers in your city, then again with a neighboring city. Note whether the practice is cited.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you do SEO for a cosmetic dentistry practice without advertising?

By writing, for each treatment, a page that explains the process, the duration, the limits, the alternatives and the fees. That is information about how you practice, which the code has allowed since the decree of December 22, 2020. What remains forbidden is obtaining a priority spot for payment or by any other means, publishing third-party testimonials and comparing yourself with other dentists.

### Can a patient from another city find my practice for veneers?

Not through the Google map, where distance is one of the three official ranking criteria. But a complete veneers page can appear in the regular results and in AI answers, which rely first on page content. That is why, in cosmetic dentistry, the website carries more weight than the Google Business Profile.

### Can you publish before-and-after photos in cosmetic dentistry?

No article of the code names them, and it would be wrong to call them banned in France, even though they are banned in Luxembourg. The National Council's recommendations of June 19, 2026 mention them in the conditional, among examples that would tend to suggest a guaranteed result. The cautious approach is not to build your communication on them, and to show instead the technical equipment, the dentists' backgrounds and how treatments unfold.
