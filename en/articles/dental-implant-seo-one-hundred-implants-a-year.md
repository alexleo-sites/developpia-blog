Take a fictional practice in Angers: two dentists, a surgical suite, a cone beam CT (CBCT), one hundred implants placed a year. Its website is a handful of pages. The "treatments" page mentions implant dentistry in one line, between cleaning and whitening. On its name, the practice ranks well. On "dental implant Angers", it doesn't appear.

This situation is common. SEO for an implant dentist follows the same rules as for any dental practice, explained on our [dental SEO](https://en.developpia.fr/dental-seo/) page. But implants change three things: the way the patient searches, what they expect from your website, and how much weight AI tools carry in their choice.

## A patient who comes from far away and compares for a long time

A patient in pain wants the nearest practice, open today. A patient considering an implant is not in a hurry. They have often been missing a tooth for months. They are willing to drive to a practice that reassures them. And they compare: several websites, Google Business Profiles, Doctolib profiles (Doctolib is France's leading online booking platform), several visits before they call.

Their questions are specific. How is the implant placed. How long does the whole treatment take. Does it hurt. Can a whole jaw be replaced. What is a bone graft. What is a cone beam CT. How much does it cost. They type these into Google, where 59.8% of French people look for health information (Eurostat, 2025). They also ask an AI tool: 56.6% of French people already use artificial intelligence, including to find a dentist (Arcom and Médiamétrie, April 2026).

For an implant, your website has to play the role of the first consultation: answer before the appointment, in the patient's words. A website that only says "we offer implant dentistry" lets that patient leave for a practice that explains.

## Patients' words, not dentists' words

The dentist says "implantology", "edentulism", "implant-supported bridge". The patient says "dental implant", "missing tooth", "permanent false tooth". "Implant dentaire" (dental implant) is searched 33,100 times a month in France (Semrush, August 2026). So the patient's word goes into your page titles, your page addresses and your Google Business Profile. The dentist's word stays in the text, with its explanation.

Around implants, patients mostly type these families of questions:

- process and duration: "how is an implant placed", "how long between the implant and the crown";
- pain and recovery: "dental implant pain", "time off work";
- scope: "implant for one tooth", "several teeth", "full jaw";
- cost: "dental implant cost", "treatment estimate", "insurance reimbursement";
- special cases and equipment: "bone graft", "implants and smoking", "cone beam CT".

Each one deserves a written answer on your website.

## The implant page: the consultation before the consultation

A complete implant page is the centerpiece of an implant practice's website. It does not replace the consultation, it prepares for it. Here is what it contains, in the order the patient reads it:

1. What an implant is, in a few simple lines.
2. The process step by step: consultation, exam and cone beam CT (a 3D X-ray that shows the bone volume), treatment plan and treatment estimate, placement, healing, abutment and crown, follow-up.
3. The total length of the process, which varies with healing and with whether a graft is needed.
4. Usual recovery: discomfort, swelling, precautions, possible time off work.
5. Alternatives: bridge, removable denture, doing nothing, with the limits of each.
6. Cases where an implant is not possible or must wait: insufficient bone, smoking, certain conditions or certain medications.
7. The practice's equipment (CBCT, surgical suite, surgical guide, digital impressions): information about how you practice, which the code of ethics allows.
8. Fees: an honest range, what it covers (implant, abutment, crown), what gets added (graft, extraction), and a reminder that the treatment estimate sets the final amount.

Information on fees is an obligation (article R.4127-240). We have covered how to present it in the article [fees on your dental practice website](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dental-fees-on-your-website-rules/). For an implant, it is the question that decides the call. Our [dental practice website](https://en.developpia.fr/dental-practice-website/) page shows where this page fits among the others.

> **The key takeaway**
> For an implant, patients choose the practice that answered their questions before they asked them. Your implant page is that answer: it informs, it sells nothing, and that is exactly what the Dental Council allows.

## One page per situation, without promising results

The principle is still [one page per treatment](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/one-page-per-treatment-dentist-website/). But a single page is not enough for a practice that places one hundred implants a year. The patient who lost one tooth and the patient who has no lower teeth left don't type the same thing, and don't have the same fears. Our fictional practice's website benefits from separating:

- the implant for a single tooth, with the choice between implant and bridge;
- implants for several teeth, with the implant-supported bridge;
- the full jaw, with a fixed or implant-stabilized prosthesis;
- bone grafting and sinus augmentation (often called a sinus lift), for patients who were told "it's not possible".

Each page follows the same structure (who it is for, the process, the duration, alternatives, fees, a link back to the main page), with its own address and a title that uses the patient's word.

One point to watch: these pages describe a process, never a result. Write "an implant replaces the root of the tooth", not "you'll get your smile back". And show the practice rather than mouths: the French Dental Council (Ordre national des chirurgiens-dentistes) cites before-and-after photos as an example of what can suggest a guaranteed result, and they are banned in Luxembourg. Photos of the surgical suite, the CBCT and the team are just as informative. The same logic applies to veneers and aligners, described in the article on [cosmetic dentistry SEO](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/cosmetic-dentistry-seo/).

## The rules: informing, yes; advertising, no

Since [decree no. 2020-1658 of December 22, 2020](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000042730959), a dentist may inform the public about their skills, background, practice conditions and fees, including on a website. Advertising remains forbidden. Communication must be fair and honest, with no third-party testimonials, no comparison with other dentists, no encouragement of unnecessary treatment (article R.4127-215-1). The guide [what the Dental Council allows on a dentist website](https://en.developpia.fr/guides/what-the-dental-council-allows-on-a-dentist-website/) details each rule.

Two points concern implant dentists directly. The title, first. The title reserved for the three recognized specialties (dentofacial orthopedics, oral surgery, oral medicine) does not cover implant dentistry. In a reminder dated August 3, 2026, the Dental Council states that a dentist may mention their main areas of practice, implant dentistry for example, without leading the public to believe they hold that title: ["un exercice limité n'est pas une spécialité"](https://www.ordre-chirurgiens-dentistes.fr/actualites/communication-un-exercice-limite-nest-pas-une-specialite/) (a limited practice is not a specialty). So write "practices implant dentistry" or "focuses their practice on implant dentistry", and cite the degrees the Dental Council recognizes. This focus does not appear on the practice's nameplate.

Testimonials, next. Patients would like to read about another patient's experience, and the code forbids it, on the website as in video. The answer lies in how precise your pages are, your equipment and your dentists' backgrounds. A dentist who regularly posts videos has a framework to know, the Dental Council's rules on [the dentist influencer](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dentist-influencer-what-the-dental-council-allows/).

## The same word everywhere: website, Google Business Profile, Doctolib

For the map, Google relies on three official criteria: relevance, distance and prominence (Google help). Distance works against you with a patient who lives far away. That leaves relevance: Google must know, beyond any doubt, that your practice places implants. That comes from consistency across your three storefronts.

On your Google Business Profile, the word "implant" appears in the services, in the description and in the questions and answers. The photos show the technical equipment. Address, phone number and hours are identical to the website's. Our [Google Business Profile for dentists](https://en.developpia.fr/google-business-profile-dentist/) page lists every field.

On Doctolib, the dentist's page often outranks the practice website when a patient types their name. Check that the profile mentions implant dentistry and links to your website. The guide [Doctolib outranks my website](https://en.developpia.fr/guides/doctolib-outranks-my-website/) explains how to take back control.

Finally, link to the implant page from the home page, the menu and each dentist's page: a page that nothing links to looks secondary.

## Your place in AI answers

A patient who compares for a long time often ends up asking an AI tool: "which practice places implants near Angers, and how does it work". Since [July 22, 2026](https://blog.google/intl/fr-fr/nouveautes-produits/explorez-obtenez-des-reponses/recherche-ia-apercus-mode/), Google has been showing its AI Overviews and AI Mode in France, which answer before the list of websites. ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity do the same. These answers rely on readable, citable pages: a complete implant page, with fees and information that match everywhere.

Two checks are essential. The bots from OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity do not read JavaScript: if your page is displayed by a script (a small program that fills in the page after it opens), it is invisible to them. And Cloudflare, a service that protects many websites, has been blocking AI bots by default since July 2025. The guide [how AI chooses a dentist](https://en.developpia.fr/guides/how-ai-chooses-a-dentist/) gives the steps to follow, and the article [is your practice showing up in ChatGPT](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/is-my-dental-practice-on-chatgpt-ten-minute-check/) lets you measure your place, as the percentage of answers in which the practice is cited, never as a ranking.

For our fictional practice, the next steps are clear: an implant page in patients' words, pages for each situation, the word "implant" everywhere, a website AI tools can read. The following project is each dentist's page, the one the patient reads just before calling.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I call myself an "implantologist" on my website?

The word is not a recognized title, and the title reserved for the three recognized specialties does not cover implant dentistry. The Dental Council accepts that a dentist mention their main areas of practice, as long as it creates no confusion with those specialties. The safe wording is "practices implant dentistry" or "focuses their practice on implant dentistry", along with the degrees the Dental Council recognizes. If in doubt, your local (departmental) Dental Council can review the page.

### Should I have one page per situation or a single implants page?

Both. The main implant page explains the implant, the process, recovery, alternatives and fees. The situation pages (single tooth, several teeth, full jaw, graft) answer specific searches and the fears specific to each case. Each page links back to the main one.

### Can a patient from another city find the practice on the Google map?

Distance is one of the three official criteria for the map, and a patient who lives far away sees practices near them first. However, a complete implant page can appear in the regular results and in AI answers, which rely first on page content. That is why the website matters more for an implant dentist than for a general practice.
