Take a fictional practice, Cabinet des Quatre Chemins, in Angers. The website was rebuilt eight months ago, the Google Business Profile is up to date, the treatment pages are written. The dentist asks the only question that really matters to them: how many patients has this brought me?

A rankings report does not answer that question. SEO results are counted in patient actions, and nearly all of those actions can be read for free, as long as you know where to look and what to write down. Here are the numbers to track, exactly where they live, and the log to keep at the front desk.

## A result is a patient in the chair, not a ranking

A position on Google changes depending on where the search is made from, the device and the time of day. Two patients on the same street do not see the same list. A report that announces a ranking therefore tells you nothing about your schedule.

The real path is simpler. A patient searches, sees your practice, takes an action (calls or requests an appointment), then comes in. Each step is counted separately. SEO works on the first two: being seen, and making the patient want to take that action. The rest depends on your front desk, your hours and the room in your schedule.

Today, this path runs through three places. 59.8% of French people look for health information online (Eurostat, 2025), Google handles 88.76% of searches in France (Statcounter, August 2026), and 56.6% of French people already use artificial intelligence, including to find a dentist (Arcom and Médiamétrie, April 2026). ## The four numbers that tell you whether something is happening

Do not track ten metrics. Four are enough, and they read in the order the patient moves forward.

- **Actions on your Google Business Profile.** Taps on the call button, direction requests and clicks to your website. This is the first place a patient in your city meets you.
- **Calls from the website.** A tap-to-call number on every page, and a count of taps on that number.
- **Appointment requests.** The website form, the button to your online booking tool, and the messages you receive.
- **New patients who mention the internet.** The only number counted in the chair, and the only one that counts in your schedule.

The first three show that the work is having an effect. The fourth tells you what it is worth. A practice that sees the first three climb without the fourth moving has a front desk or appointment wait-time problem, not an SEO problem.

## Where to read each number, and what it does not tell you

Each number has a specific, free place to find it, and a limit you need to know before you look at it.

| What you count | Where to read it | What it does not tell you |
|---|---|---|
| Calls, directions and website clicks from the profile | Your Google Business Profile performance, in Google or the Maps app | Whether the call was answered, or whether it led to an appointment |
| Searches that show the profile | The profile's search terms report | Who searched, or from which neighborhood |
| Website impressions and clicks | Search Console, Google's free tool | Whether the visitor called after reading the page |
| Calls from the website | The count of taps on the tap-to-call number | Patients who copy the number by hand |
| Online appointment requests | The website form and your booking tool | Canceled appointments or no-shows |
| New patients who came from the internet | The front desk tracking sheet | What triggered the search, often a friend or relative |
| Presence in AI answers | Your own questions to AI tools, repeated every month | How many patients came from them |

No single row is enough on its own. Reading all seven together, month after month, is what gives an accurate picture. Google explains in its help center [how to read your Business Profile performance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en), and our [Google Business Profile for dentists](https://en.developpia.fr/google-business-profile-dentist/) page details the fields that increase these actions.

## The front desk sheet: five steps that change everything

The most useful number is not in any tool. It is written down at the front desk, in one second per new patient.

1. Print one sheet per month, with four columns: the date, the treatment requested, the answer to "how did you find us?", and a box for "existing patient".
2. Ask the question only once, when creating the patient's file, never over the phone in the middle of an emergency.
3. Put each answer into one of four boxes: internet, a friend or relative, a fellow dentist, walking past the practice. Write "internet" also when the patient says Google, Maps, your website, ChatGPT or your booking tool.
4. At the end of the month, count the lines in each box. Five minutes is enough.
5. Keep the sheets. After a year, you compare each month with the same month of the previous year, which is the only honest comparison.

This sheet is worth more than any paid tool, because it measures what happens after the click. Sometimes it also shows that patients come mostly through word of mouth, and that the website's job is then to reassure rather than to help people discover you.

> **The sentence to remember**
> SEO is measured in only four places: actions on your Google Business Profile, calls from the website, appointment requests and new patients who say they came from the internet. Everything you read elsewhere helps you understand, never judge.

## How many patients: the honest count, month after month

The question "how many patients" has an answer, but it requires two precautions.

The first is to separate patients who were looking for you from those who discovered you. A patient who types the practice's name already knew you: they found you, they did not discover you. A patient who types a treatment and the name of their city did not know you. These are two different results, and only the second measures the visibility you gained. The search terms report on your Google Business Profile makes this split for you.

The second is to compare with the same month of the previous year. An August dip is not a decline, and a busy September is not a victory. Practices that compare with the previous month celebrate and worry for nothing.

The calculation then fits in one sentence: the number of new patients in the month who answered "internet", minus those who were already searching for your name, compared with the same month of the previous year. Nothing more. This number rises slowly, and it rises in steps, for the reasons explained in our article on [how long dental SEO takes](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/how-long-dental-seo-takes/).

## The ten-minute check to do yourself

Before measuring, make sure the patient's action is possible and counted. Take your phone, turn off Wi-Fi, and go through these six points.

1. Search for your practice's name, open your profile and tap the call button. The phone must dial the practice's number, not another one.
2. Open your website from the profile. Count the seconds before you see text.
3. On the home page, look for the phone number without scrolling. If you have to look for it, so does the patient.
4. Tap that number. If it does not dial on its own, it is written as an image or plain text, and nobody is counting it.
5. Open a treatment page and repeat the number test. Many websites only show it on the home page.
6. Ask an AI which dentist to see for your main treatment in your city, and check whether your practice is mentioned. The full method is in our article [is my dental practice on ChatGPT? a ten-minute check](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/is-my-dental-practice-on-chatgpt-ten-minute-check/).

A single failed point out of these six skews the whole month's measurement. ## What no number will tell you, and what no one can promise you

Three things will always stay out of reach. The share of word of mouth triggered by a search, since a patient often checks online a practice a friend recommended. The patient who saw your profile, remembered your name and called three weeks later. And the number of people who read an AI answer mentioning your practice, since those answers cannot be counted.

There is also a legal limit. Article R.4127-217 II of the French Public Health Code (code de la santé publique) prohibits a dentist from obtaining, for payment or by any other means, a listing that would display their information with priority. The decree is [available on Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000042730959), the official French legal database. A provider who promises a ranking, or a number of patients per month, is therefore promising either something they do not control, or something that puts your liability on the line, not theirs. What you can ask for is a monthly report of these four numbers, with the comparison to the previous year.

If your numbers have not moved in several months, the cause is rarely the measurement. ## Frequently asked questions

### How many patients does SEO bring a dental practice?

No general number makes sense, because it depends on your city, your treatments, your competition and your schedule. What can be measured is the number of new patients in the month who answer "internet" at the front desk, minus those who were already searching for the practice's name, compared with the same month of the previous year. It is the only honest calculation, and it requires having kept the sheet for at least a year.

### How do I know whether a new patient came from Google?

By asking at the front desk when creating the patient's file, and putting the answer into four boxes: internet, a friend or relative, a fellow dentist, walking past the practice. Your Google Business Profile performance gives you the number of calls and direction requests alongside it, but it never says whether a call led to an appointment. The two measurements complement each other.

### Do I need a paid tool to measure dental SEO results?

No. Your Google Business Profile performance, Search Console and a tracking sheet at the front desk cover the four useful numbers, and they are free. A paid tool becomes worthwhile later, when you want to track the source of each call page by page, which already requires a website where the number is tap-to-call everywhere.
