Take a fictional practice, Cabinet dentaire des Halles, in Niort. The website is six years old. Both dentists dislike it, a colleague has shown them theirs, and a vendor has just sent over a redesign proposal. Should they rebuild everything, or fix what they have?

The question comes up often, and the answer is almost never a matter of taste. A website can look dated and be well built. Another can look great and be invisible to Google and to AI alike. This article gives you the framework to decide, without a quote and without an outside opinion.

## Redesign or fix: two different problems

Fixing a website means keeping its address, its pages and its history, and changing what is wrong: a piece of text, a missing page, images that are too heavy, a prohibited statement. The work shows within a few days and puts nothing at risk.

Redesigning a website means starting over with a new build. Sometimes it is the only option, but it is also the most delicate moment in a website's life: page addresses change, and a page whose address changes without a [redirect](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dental-seo-glossary/#redirect) disappears from the results.

So the right question is not "is my website attractive?" but "can what bothers me be fixed on this foundation?" Five questions answer it.

## The five questions that settle it

1. **Is the text of my pages actually in the page?** Open a treatment page, right-click, choose "View page source," then search for a sentence from your text. If it is not there, your content is displayed by a script, a small program that fills in the page after it opens. The bots from OpenAI, Anthropic and Perplexity do not read that language: they see an empty page. Our article on [how AI reads a dentist website](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/why-ai-cannot-read-your-dental-website/) covers this in detail. A website built this way cannot be fixed page by page.
2. **Does the website load quickly on a phone?** Measure it with Google's free tool, [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/?hl=en), on the mobile tab. If the slowness comes from oversized photos uploaded straight from a camera, it can be fixed in a morning. If it comes from a theme loaded with effects and stacked plugins, it is in the build.
3. **Do I have one page per treatment?** Count your treatment pages. A single "our treatments" page that lists ten procedures in ten lines does not answer any specific search. If your tool lets you add pages and give them a title, that is a fix, not a redesign. The outline of a treatment page is described in our article on [one page per treatment](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/one-page-per-treatment-dentist-website/).
4. **Can I change a sentence myself today?** Try, right now, to correct your opening hours. If you do not have access, if the vendor no longer answers, or if the domain name is registered in their name rather than yours, the problem is not the website: it is control over it. No lasting fix is possible until that is settled.
5. **Does my website put me at risk with the French Dental Council (Ordre national des chirurgiens-dentistes)?** Look for a patient testimonial, a before-and-after photo, a "website by" credit, or missing fees. These four points get fixed today, whatever you decide about the rest, and they come before everything else. The [seven mistakes on dental practice websites](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/seven-mistakes-on-dental-practice-websites/) give you the test for each one.

## The decision table

| What you notice | What it means | The decision |
|---|---|---|
| The text does not appear in the page source | The build hides the content from search engines and AI | Redesign |
| The website is slow because of the photos | Image weight, not the build | Fix |
| The website is slow even without photos | The theme and plugins are weighing it down | Redesign |
| A single "our treatments" page | Pages are missing, not a website | Fix |
| Impossible to add a page or edit a title | The tool no longer keeps up with your practice | Redesign |
| The domain name is not in your name | An ownership problem, not a website problem | Settle it first |
| Testimonials, before-and-after photos, missing fees | A risk under the code of ethics | Fix right away |
| The website is unreadable on a phone, with text you have to zoom in on | Usually the build | Redesign |
| You dislike the website, but everything else is fine | A question of appearance | Fix |

Two or more "Redesign" rows, and a redesign is justified. Only one, and you fix it and keep what you have built up.

> **The one thing to remember**
> A website is not redesigned because it looks dated, but because its build makes it impossible to fix. As long as you can add a page, write text and make it lighter, a fix costs less time and puts no patient at risk of losing track of you.

## The check to run yourself, in fifteen minutes

Grab your phone and a computer, use a private browsing window, and follow this order:

1. Type the practice name into Google. Note what comes up before your website.
2. Type a treatment you offer and the name of your town. See whether your website appears on the first page.
3. Open your website on 4G, with Wi-Fi off, and count the seconds before you see the text.
4. Look for the button to call. If you have to scroll or zoom, note it.
5. View the source of a treatment page and search for one of your sentences.
6. Run that page's address through PageSpeed Insights, mobile tab.
7. List your treatment pages, one by one.
8. Ask an AI which dentist places implants in your town, and see whether the practice is mentioned.

These eight points fit on one sheet of paper. It is the same assessment we run before telling a practice whether to redesign or fix. 59.8% of people in France look for health information online (Eurostat, 2025), and 56.6% of people in France already use artificial intelligence, including to find a dentist (Arcom and Médiamétrie, April 2026): both paths matter.

## What you keep, either way

A redesign never starts from scratch. These elements are kept, and losing them is what costs the most:

- the domain name, which must be registered in the name of the dentist or the practice entity;
- the addresses of the pages that get visits, or failing that, a redirect from each one to its new page;
- the text you have already written, especially the treatment pages and the fees page;
- the photos of the practice, the team and the equipment;
- the Google Business Profile, which does not depend on the website and must definitely not be recreated;
- the address, phone number and hours, written the same way everywhere.

## If you redesign: the order that protects your SEO

A redesign is prepared before the new website goes live.

1. List every current page address, including the ones nothing links to anymore.
2. For each one, decide which page will replace it. Google asks for a permanent redirect to a page with equivalent content, and advises against sending everyone to the home page, as explained in its [official guide to site moves](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/site-move-with-url-changes?hl=en).
3. Start from the existing text before writing new text. A page that ranks well should be rewritten with care. If the website has a blog, the test for whether [a dental practice blog is worth it](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dental-practice-blog-worth-it/) helps you sort out which articles to keep.
4. Launch the new website on a quiet day, never the day before a vacation.
5. Check each redirect one by one, from a phone.
6. Keep those redirects for at least a year: that is the minimum Google recommends.
7. Look at visits and calls every week for two months.

A drop in visits during the first few weeks is common. It recovers if the redirects are right. ## During the work, the Dental Council's rules do not change

A redesign is the right time to bring the website back in line, not to drift away from them. The new website must still inform patients about your fees and payment methods, as required by article R.4127-240 II of the French Public Health Code, which you can read [on Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000042795939). It must still have no third-party testimonials, no comparison with other dentists and no before-and-after photos. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does redesigning a dental practice website mean losing your SEO?

Not if the old page addresses point to the new ones through a permanent redirect. What loses visits is a page that changes address without a redirect, or a redirect that sends everything to the home page. Google asks you to keep these redirects for at least a year.

### How often should a dental practice website be redesigned?

There is no set lifespan. A well-built website can be fixed and expanded for years. What triggers a redesign is text that search engines and AI cannot see, slowness that comes from the build, or a tool that no longer lets you add a page.

### Can a dentist website be fixed without changing how it looks?

Yes, and it is often the most useful order. Adding one page per treatment, writing out your fees as text, lightening images and removing prohibited statements can all be done without touching the design. Appearance comes next, once the content and speed are solid.
