## Why nobody can give you a date

It is the first question every dentist asks, and the hardest one to answer honestly. Google does not publish a schedule. It does not say when it rereads a page, or when it reshuffles its results. Two practices doing the same work in two different cities will not move at the same pace.

So the honest answer is not a date. It is a description in three parts: what moves fast, what takes months, and what is in no one's hands. Our [dental SEO](https://en.developpia.fr/dental-seo/) page covers the three places a patient finds you: Google's results, the map and AI answers. Each one moves at its own pace.

## What moves fast

Some things show up within a few days or a few weeks.

- Google discovering a clean website. Google does not guess that a site exists: you have to tell it, by submitting your sitemap in [Search Console](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dental-seo-glossary/#search-console), Google's free tool that shows what Google sees of your site. If the site is fast, readable on a phone and error-free, its pages get into Google's index within a few days to a few weeks. At that point they are known to Google, which is not yet the same thing as ranking well.
- Searches for your name. A patient who types the practice name should land on you. When the website, the Google Business Profile and the directories all show the same name, the same address and the same phone number, that result comes quickly. If a booking directory outranks your own website on your own name, the guide [Doctolib outranks my website](https://en.developpia.fr/guides/doctolib-outranks-my-website/) (Doctolib is France's leading online booking platform) explains why and what to do about it.
- A corrected Google Business Profile. Category, hours, photos, description: these edits are picked up quickly, and they immediately change what patients see on the map. Our [Google Business Profile for dentists](https://en.developpia.fr/google-business-profile-dentist/) page walks through every field.

None of these three brings in a patient who is looking for a treatment and does not know you yet. They make sure that someone looking for you finds you.

## What takes months

The real prize is searches that combine a treatment and a city: "dental implant Lyon", "veneers Bordeaux". The practices already ranking well have often had a website for years and a well-fed profile for just as long. Google trusts them because it knows them. A new website, even a very good one, starts with less history.

Two things are built slowly:

1. Trust earned by content. Google compares your implants page with the ones that already exist in your city. It checks whether it answers the patient's questions, whether it is complete, whether it is kept up to date. One page per treatment, written with care, gains ground page after page.
2. Trust coming from outside. Mentions of your practice on other websites, the Dental Council's directory, health directories, the town hall, a local association, the local press: these are inbound links (also called "backlinks"). You cannot simply order them: they come over time, when your information is accurate and easy to reuse.

These are the searches where we talk in months. Not because the work takes long to do, but because Google takes time to grant its trust, and because the practices ranked above you are not standing still.

For a sense of scale, measured across the whole web: **72.9% of the pages in Google's top ten results are more than three years old, and the page ranked first is five years old on average** (Ahrefs, study published May 15, 2025, data from fall 2023). That figure covers every kind of search, including the most fiercely contested national ones. "Dentist" plus a neighborhood is far less competitive than "car insurance": a practice moves faster than that average. But the unit of measure is the same, and it is not the week.

## What is in no one's hands

Three things are out of your control, and out of your provider's control too.

- Google updates. Google regularly changes the way it ranks pages. Some sites go up, others go down, without having changed a thing. Since [July 22, 2026](https://blog.google/intl/fr-fr/nouveautes-produits/explorez-obtenez-des-reponses/recherche-ia-apercus-mode/), Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode have been live in France: the answer appears before the list of websites. The guide [Google's AI Overviews and your practice](https://en.developpia.fr/guides/google-ai-overviews-dentist/) covers this.
- Local competition. A practice opening two streets away, a colleague redoing their website, a dental center moving into town: any of them can change the order of the results in your city.
- Seasons. A summer dip is not a setback, and a back-to-school spike is not a victory. Compare a month with the same month the year before, not with the previous month.

Anyone who promises you a result by a fixed date is therefore claiming control over three things they do not control.

## Why the Dental Council forbids promising a ranking

The dentists' code of ethics does more than ban advertising. Since decree no. 2020-1658 of December 22, 2020, article R.4127-217 of the French Public Health Code forbids a dentist from obtaining, for payment or by any other means, a digital listing that would make their information appear first in search results. The French Dental Council (Ordre national des chirurgiens-dentistes) sums this point up in a short phrase: "paid or not". The text of the decree is [on Légifrance](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/jorf/id/JORFTEXT000042730959).

A provider who promises you "number one in 30 days" is therefore putting you in an awkward position. Either the promise is empty, and you are paying for a sentence. Or it relies on a way of obtaining priority placement, and it is the dentist, not the provider, who is bound by the code. Either way, the promise is a sign to walk away. The right questions to ask are in the article [choosing between an agency, a freelancer or doing it yourself](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dental-seo-agency-freelancer-or-diy/).

What the code allows is information: a clear website, an accurate profile, published fees. The guide [what the Dental Council allows on a dentist website](https://en.developpia.fr/guides/what-the-dental-council-allows-on-a-dentist-website/) covers all of these rules.

> **The key takeaway**
> Nobody can give you a date, and the code of ethics forbids anyone from promising you a ranking. What you can demand is honest tracking: the pages Google knows, impressions, clicks, then phone calls.

## How to track progress without looking at a ranking

A ranking is a poor marker. It changes depending on the city the search comes from, the device, the time of day and the person's search history. Here are the markers you can read without getting it wrong, in the order in which they move.

1. Pages known to Google. In Search Console, the indexing report tells you which pages of your site are in the index and which are not yet. It is the first sign the work is being seen. Google explains [how to get started with this tool](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9128668?hl=en) in its help center.
2. Impressions. This is the number of times one of your pages appeared in the results. Impressions rise before clicks do. When they rise on "implant" plus your city, Google is starting to consider you for that search.
3. Clicks. The patient saw your page and opened it. The report shows which searches it came from.
4. Phone calls and appointment requests. A clearly visible number on every page, a booking button, and one simple question asked at the front desk: "how did you find us?".

Look at a ranking to understand, never to judge. To count what these markers bring to the chair, the article on [dental SEO results in number of patients](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dental-seo-results-how-many-new-patients/) details the four numbers and the front desk tracking sheet.

## A fictional practice, step by step

Take a fictional practice, Dr. Morel's in Tours, which does not exist. The website is several years old, the Google Business Profile has a vague category, and the fees are nowhere to be found. Here are the steps it goes through, with no results figures, because they would be made up.

Step one, the foundation. The new website goes live, with one page per treatment and a fees page. The profile is corrected. The sitemap is submitted in Search Console. The first pages appear in the indexing report, and a search for "Dr. Morel Tours" brings up the website first.

Step two, the first impressions. The report starts showing impressions on "dental implant Tours", far down the results. Nobody clicks yet: Google is testing the page.

Step three, the first clicks. They come first from specific searches, like "dental implant cost Tours", and later from broader ones. The practice adds to each page the questions patients ask on the phone.

Step four, the plateau and the dips. A Google update pushes some pages down, then brings them back. Summer hollows out impressions. The practice changes nothing in its approach and compares with the previous year.

Step five, the phone calls. The dental assistant notes that new patients mention the website and the map. It is the only step that counts, and you cannot read it in Google.

The practice did not wait for a ranking: it watched the right markers and kept going.

## What really speeds things up

Nothing forces Google's hand. But four things make its job easier, and that is what shortens the timeline.

- Consistency over time. A page improved every month, an updated profile, a patient question added: Google rereads a living website more often.
- Consistent information. The same name, the same address, the same phone number and the same hours on the website, the profile, the Dental Council's directory and Doctolib. Every mismatch forces Google to doubt, and doubt slows everything down.
- One page per treatment. A single "our treatments" page cannot answer "implant", "veneers" and "crown" all at once. Our [dental practice website](https://en.developpia.fr/dental-practice-website/) page shows how these pages are built.
- Published fees. It is an obligation under the code (R.4127-240 II), and it is also what patients and AI tools look for. The article [fees on your dental practice website](https://en.developpia.fr/blog/dental-fees-on-your-website-rules/) explains how to build that page.

These four habits do not set a date. They make each month of work add to the one before, instead of starting from scratch. The logical next step, once these markers are in place, is to also measure your presence in AI search, where the question is no longer your ranking but your share of answers.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take a new dental practice website to show up on Google?

Once the sitemap is submitted in Search Console, Google reads a clean website within a few days to a few weeks, and its pages get into the index. At that point you can be found by the practice name. Being found for "implant" plus your city takes much longer, and that timeline depends on your city, the competition and how consistently the work is done.

### Can a provider guarantee the top spot on Google?

No. Google does not sell spots in its organic results, and for a dentist, article R.4127-217 forbids obtaining a priority listing, paid or not. A ranking guarantee is therefore either empty or outside the rules. Ask instead how the provider measures progress, month after month.

### How can I tell if my practice's SEO is making progress?

Open Search Console, Google's free tool. Look first at the number of indexed pages, then at impressions and clicks, search by search, comparing with the same month the previous year. Also have the front desk note how each new patient found you: that is the final marker.
